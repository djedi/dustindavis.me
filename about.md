# Hello, I'm Dustin. 👋

I'm a software engineer who grew up in a small rural Utah town, left to see the
world, and came right back to a small rural Utah town. I build things, I break
things, and then I write about how I fixed them so future-me doesn't have to
figure it out twice.

Here's what made me, me.

## Baseball first, everything else second ⚾️

I've loved baseball my whole life. My dad coached football, wrestling, and
baseball, and my brother and I spent a lot of miles on the team bus. I played
football, basketball, and baseball in high school, and finished my senior year
by breaking my arm pitching in the state tournament — [Dave
Dravecky](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EzLtowZNrmE) style. Worth it.

These days my sport of choice is pickleball, but baseball never left me. I'll
watch it, coach it, score it, and I still have a soft spot for the boys of
summer.

## Movies, and a lot of them 🎬

I'm a big movie guy. A *lot* of movies. My two genres are romantic comedies and
baseball movies, and I'm not ashamed of either. There's a special place in my
heart for a good rom-com, and for a baseball movie that understands the game —
that it's equal parts failure, redemption, and doing it again tomorrow.

## Audiobooks (mostly non-fiction) 🎧

I'm nearly always listening to an audiobook, and it's almost always
non-fiction. I want to know how things work, how people think, and how to get
better at being a human.

Two books sit above the rest:

- The best book I've ever read is the **Book of Mormon**.
- The book that gave me my life back is **The MindBody Prescription** by Dr.
  John Sarno — it changed how I understood my own body and pain, and I don't
  exaggerate when I say it turned my life around.

## Faith matters to me more than anything ✝️

I'm a member of [The Church of Jesus Christ of
Latter-day Saints](https://churchofjesuschrist.org), and it is a big deal to me.
It shapes who I am, how I see people, and how I spend my Sundays and my life.

Between growing up and settling down, I served a two-year mission for the Church
in **[Cape Town, South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town)** 🇿🇦. It
was life-changing. It taught me to love people I'd never have met otherwise, to
work without expecting credit, and that a homegrown kid from a rural Utah town
can belong anywhere.

## I love to build 🔨

Making things is my default setting. Software, furniture, plans, systems —
doesn't matter. If it can be built, I want to build it, and I want to build it
well. I've spent my career at companies like IHC Health Plans, 3M Health
Information Systems, and a few great places in between (see my
[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dustindavis/)), and I picked up a degree
in Computer Information Systems from Weber State along the way.

## I love to automate things 🤖

If I do something twice, I'll spend an afternoon making a script do it forever.
I love automating the boring, the repetitive, and the error-prone — and then
handing the time I saved back to the things I actually care about.

## I love bleeding edge technology ✨

I'm a sucker for the new and shiny. If it just shipped and nobody's using it
yet, I want to try it. I love being early, learning the sharp edges, and
figuring out what the future is going to look like before it's obvious to
everyone else.

## I'm taking up gardening 🥕

Newest adventure: growing things. I'm taking up gardening and learning that,
unlike software, you can't just `git revert` a frost. It's humbling, and I love
it.

## Back home in a small rural Utah town 🏔️

The world is a big place, and I've seen a good chunk of it. But I've come back
around to where I started — living in a small rural Utah town with my beautiful
wife Rachel, our five kids, and our dog. Quiet, spacious, and full of the things
that matter.

## Why blog?

More than anything, I blog for myself. I can't tell you how often I've gone back
to my own blog to remember how I did something. It's also fun when a co-worker
says they found an answer on my blog — my response is always, "I would have just
sent you to my blog."

I hope you find something useful here, whether it's for work or for home. Say
hello on [X](https://x.com/dustindavis/) if you'd like — I'd love to hear from
you.
